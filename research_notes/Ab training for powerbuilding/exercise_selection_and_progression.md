## Anatomy and function: which movements train which abdominal muscles

### Takeaway
The rectus abdominis (RA) mainly flexes the trunk (crunch-type movements) and resists trunk extension (rollouts, planks). The obliques add lateral flexion and rotation, and they resist both (side planks, suitcase carries, Pallof press). The internal oblique (IO) and transverse abdominis (TrA) are the main bracing and pressure muscles, and squats already train them hard. Upper and lower RA can be biased slightly by exercise choice, but the one hypertrophy study found offered no evidence that this matters for growth.

### Cited Findings
- **Exercise choice shifts activity between muscles.** An EMG study (SciELO, *Fisioterapia em Movimento*, 2024) compared the back squat with localized trunk exercises. It concluded that squats are an effective exercise for the iliocostalis, multifidus and internal oblique. Localized exercises suited the external oblique and RA better. — [Fisioter. Mov. (SciELO) "The squat exercise recruits core muscles as much as localized exercises"](https://www.scielo.br/j/fm/a/fzmbcFwdGtRGxJCWHbqcwGn/?lang=en); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377427367_The_squat_exercise_recruits_core_muscles_as_much_as_localized_exercises)
- **Upper vs lower RA activation (acute).** A 2023 diagnostic-ultrasound study's title reports "preferential activation of rectus abdominis segments with exercises targeting upper versus lower segments". The RA shortened and thickened in every exercise tested and changed most in the abdominal crunch. — [Diagnostic Ultrasound Shows Preferential Activation of RA Segments… (PMC10824285)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10824285/)
- **Upper vs lower RA growth.** A trial in 36 resistance-trained women ran different direct-ab protocols for 9 weeks and measured RA cross-sectional area by ultrasound. It concluded that "the manipulation of the exercise does not generate regionally different hypertrophy responses in the RA". The year and group-level numbers could not be retrieved. — [UFMG repository thesis](https://repositorio.ufmg.br//bitstreams/ab000156-31a6-433f-8329-250395b2300a/download); [handle page](https://repositorio.ufmg.br/handle/1843/80572)
- **Squat variation changes deep-wall thickening.** In college athletes, hip-adduction isometric squats raised the TrA and IO thickness ratio more than standard squats. — [Lee et al. 2017, Isokinetics & Exercise Science](https://doi.org/10.3233/IES-171102)

### Inferences
- A useful way to pick exercises is one per function. Spinal flexion (loaded crunch type) targets the RA. Anti-extension (rollout) hits the RA and obliques isometrically or eccentrically. Rotation or anti-rotation (Pallof, cable chop) and lateral flexion or anti-lateral flexion (side plank, suitcase carry, cable side bend) target the obliques. Bracing for the TrA and IO is largely covered by heavy squats and deadlifts.
- "Lower abs" exercises such as reverse crunches and leg raises tilt EMG toward the lower RA, but given the null regional-hypertrophy result, picking exercises to grow the "lower abs" separately is not evidence-based.

### Gaps
- No full text was retrieved for the regional-hypertrophy thesis: no exact CSA changes, no publication year, and no detail on its control group.
- No dedicated hypertrophy data were found for the obliques or TrA in trained lifters.

## EMG comparisons of common ab exercises, and the limits of EMG

### Takeaway
The EMG literature consistently puts loaded or long-lever exercises at or above the plain crunch for RA activity. These include rollouts and ab wheels, hanging or captain's-chair knee raises, and reverse crunches. Hanging raises and rollouts also bring in large amounts of hip-flexor (rectus femoris) activity. Planks and side planks generally score lower for the RA. Oblique-dominant movements such as captain's chair and bicycle crunch score highest for the obliques. EMG measures acute activation, not growth, and the studies used untrained people doing bodyweight sets of about 5 reps.

### Cited Findings
- **Escamilla et al. 2006, *Physical Therapy*.** Traditional and nontraditional exercises were compared. The Power Wheel (a rollout device), hanging knee-up with straps, and a reverse crunch inclined 30° were "the most effective in activating abdominal musculature but also were the most effective in activating extraneous musculature". The authors flagged high rectus femoris activity as a possible concern for people with low-back problems. — [PubMed 16649890](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16649890/); [CSUS scholars record](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Electromyographic-Analysis-of-Traditional-and-Nontraditional/99257847129401671)
- **Escamilla et al. 2006, *JOSPT*.** In 14 subjects doing 5 reps per exercise, upper and lower RA activity was greatest for the Ab Slide, Torso Track, crunch and Ab Roller, so the crunch ranked among the best. — [PubMed 16494072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16494072/)
- **Stenger (UW–La Crosse thesis).** The Ab Wheel, side plank, Ab Circle Pro and front plank produced significantly less upper and lower RA activation than the traditional crunch. The author called the arms-crossed crunch an excellent RA exercise. This partly contradicts Escamilla, where rollout devices ranked high; differences in device and technique are a likely explanation. — [MINDS@UW](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/13f23340-bc92-4049-9c47-6fcc60df9852/full)
- **ACE-commissioned study (Anders/Porcari, *ACE FitnessMatters*, May/June 2001).** Thirteen exercises were ranked against the traditional crunch (100%). **[recalled, unverified this session]**: for the RA, bicycle maneuver was about 248% and captain's chair about 212%, with exercise-ball crunch, vertical leg crunch, Torso Track and long-arm crunch above 100%. For the obliques, captain's chair was about 310% and bicycle about 290%. Ab Roller and plank-type "hover" scored near the crunch. Gadget devices such as ab rockers scored below it. The subjects were healthy adults, untrained to recreationally active, and there was no external load. — [ACE study PDF](https://www.acefitness.org/getfit/studies/bestworstabexercises.pdf)
- **Squat and deadlift vs instability exercises (Hamlyn, Behm & Young 2007, JSCR).** At 80% 1RM, squats and deadlifts produced more erector spinae activity than bodyweight versions, the superman and the side bridge. The authors concluded that resisted, upright, dynamic lifts reach high trunk activation without instability devices. **[recalled, unverified this session]**: the abstract reported no significant RA or external-oblique differences between conditions, meaning the gains were in the back extensors, not the abs. — [PubMed 18076231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18076231/); [Memorial Univ. thesis PDF](https://research.library.mun.ca/10606/1/Hamlyn_Nicolle.pdf)
- **Nuzzo et al. 2008, JSCR.** Trunk muscle activity during squats and deadlifts was at least as high as during stability-ball exercises. The authors noted that ball exercises may not provide enough stimulus for strength or hypertrophy. — [QxMD abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/18296961/trunk-muscle-activity-during-stability-ball-and-free-weight-exercises); [full-text PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/trunk-muscle-activity-during-stability-ball-and-free-weight-2pq4cyp0mo.pdf)
- **Instruction and load matter.** A JSCR study is indexed on how "instruction, surface stability, and load intensity" affect trunk muscle activity. Bracing or hollowing cues change EMG independently of exercise choice. — [PubMed 19054687](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19054687/)
- **Squat load and position change trunk EMG.** — [Influence of Loads and Loading Position on Trunk and Lower-Extremity Muscle Activity During Squat (PMC9602963)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9602963/)
- **Training status changes trunk EMG.** Trained and untrained lifters showed different trunk activation across a fatiguing set of heavy back squats. — [PMC7862542](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7862542/)

### Inferences
- Limits of EMG as a hypertrophy predictor:
  1. Most ab EMG studies use untrained or recreational subjects, bodyweight only, and about 5 reps. They say little about a loaded set of 10–15 cable crunches taken near failure.
  2. Normalizing to MVIC and placing surface electrodes on a wall with variable fat thickness adds noise.
  3. Peak EMG ignores time under load, the muscle length where tension is highest, and fatigue across a set.
  4. "Extraneous" activity, mainly hip flexors, inflates the apparent difficulty of hanging raises and rollouts without adding to the RA stimulus.
  5. EMG rankings differ between labs: the ab wheel ranks high in Escamilla's work and low in Stenger's.

  Overall, EMG is useful for ruling out weak options such as gadgets and gentle planks, but not for picking a winner among loaded flexion movements. **[opinion/consensus, consistent with the sources above]**
- For an intermediate lifter, the movements that sit highest on EMG and can also be progressively loaded are the cable or machine crunch, weighted decline sit-up or crunch, hanging knee or leg raise, and ab-wheel rollout.

### Gaps
- No primary EMG study was found that compares the cable kneeling crunch, ab-crunch machine, Pallof press, dead bug and suitcase carry side by side in trained lifters.
- The ACE percentages and the Hamlyn RA/EO result could not be re-verified because the full-text hosts were blocked.

## What the hypertrophy and training studies show about growing the abs

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on ab growth is sparse. Ab exercise does not spot-reduce belly fat (Vispute 2011), so visible abs depend on body fat. One trial in trained women found that different direct-ab exercises produced similar, non-regional RA growth. No controlled trial was found that compares squats and deadlifts alone with squats and deadlifts plus direct ab work for RA hypertrophy. The idea that "lengthened-position" ab training builds more muscle is extrapolated from limb-muscle studies and has not been tested on the abs.

### Cited Findings
- **Vispute et al. 2011, JSCR 25(9):2559–2564.** Twenty-four adults were randomized to a control group or an ab-training group. The training group did 7 ab exercises, 2×10, 5 days a week, for 6 weeks, with diet held constant. Ab training had no significant effect on body weight, body fat %, abdominal fat or waist circumference, though endurance improved compared with control. — [Nassis summary blog](https://georgenassis.blogspot.com/2011/09/does-abdominal-exercise-training-reduce.html); [IDEA Fit summary](https://www.ideafit.com/crunches-donrsquot-cut-fat)
- **Related fat-loss studies.** Adding abdominal resistance training to diet in obese women did not reduce abdominal subcutaneous fat more than diet alone (2015 RCT). A 12-week program of aerobic plus abdominal training in sedentary adults reduced waist circumference, but the aerobic work makes the ab-specific effect impossible to separate. — [PubMed 25766455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25766455/); [PubMed 24839733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24839733/)
- **RA hypertrophy in trained women.** Different direct-ab exercises over 9 weeks did not produce regionally different RA growth, measured by ultrasound CSA. — [UFMG thesis](https://repositorio.ufmg.br//bitstreams/ab000156-31a6-433f-8329-250395b2300a/download)
- **General vs core-stability exercise.** In people with chronic low-back pain, RA thickness increased only in the general-exercise group, not the core-stability group. This suggests low-load stability work is a weak growth stimulus for the RA. — [PubMed 27253335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27253335/)
- **Null results with low-load or non-resistance methods.** A 12-week electrical-stimulation study in trained athletes, and a core-training study in junior soccer players, found no significant change in RA or oblique cross-sectional area or muscularity. — [search summary pointing to PMC6226075 / related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6226075)
- **Lengthened-position evidence (limbs only).** In trained individuals, lengthened partial reps produced muscle adaptations similar to full-ROM reps. Reviews report that, with one exception, training at longer muscle lengths produced more hypertrophy in the muscles studied (quads, calves, etc.). No study tested this in the RA. — [Lengthened partial reps in trained individuals (PMC11829627)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11829627/)
- **Cable crunch "stretch" claim.** The suggestion that the cable crunch lets you extend slightly past neutral at the top of the eccentric for a stretch stimulus comes from commercial blogs, not trials. — [Pocket-Fit cable rope crunch page](https://www.pocket-fit.app/exercises/cable-rope-crunch)
- **Registered trial.** CORE-TRAIN (ISRCTN13174817), registered March 2025, compares types of core training and was recruiting at the time indexed, with no results yet. — [ISRCTN13174817](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN13174817)

### Inferences
- The RA is a skeletal muscle with no known reason to respond differently to loading than limb muscles. Applying general hypertrophy principles is reasonable: hard sets near failure, progressive load, and roughly 10+ weekly sets for a priority muscle. Exercises that load the RA in a stretched position are a reasonable but unproven choice, e.g. a cable crunch starting from slight spinal extension, a stability-ball crunch, or a decline sit-up from full extension. **[opinion/inference]**
- Visible "abs" for a powerbuilder depend mainly on body-fat level. Direct ab work adds muscle thickness but does not remove local fat (Vispute).

### Gaps
- No controlled trial was found testing whether squats and deadlifts alone grow the RA, or comparing them with added direct ab work.
- No study was found on loaded crunches vs bodyweight crunches for RA hypertrophy, or on rep ranges specific to the abs.
- No ab-specific lengthened vs shortened trial was found.

## Do heavy compound lifts already train the abs enough?

### Takeaway
Probably not for maximal RA or external-oblique development. Heavy squats and deadlifts strongly train the spinal erectors, multifidus, IO and TrA. RA and external-oblique activity during these lifts is generally modest and no higher than during dedicated ab exercises. One older study is often cited for the opposite view, but that claim could not be confirmed. For bracing strength, the compounds already provide a large stimulus.

### Cited Findings
- **Squat vs localized exercises (EMG).** Squats were effective for iliocostalis, multifidus and IO. Localized exercises suited the external oblique and RA better. — [SciELO 2024](https://www.scielo.br/j/fm/a/fzmbcFwdGtRGxJCWHbqcwGn/?lang=en)
- **Heavy lifts vs instability exercises.** Heavy squats and deadlifts at 80% 1RM beat instability exercises for erector spinae activity, and squat and deadlift trunk activity was "at least as high as" stability-ball exercises. — [Hamlyn 2007, PubMed 18076231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18076231/); [Nuzzo 2008](https://read.qxmd.com/read/18296961/trunk-muscle-activity-during-stability-ball-and-free-weight-exercises)
- **Conflicting secondary claims.** Several blogs report weak RA activation in deadlifts, even less in squats, and higher RA activity in the overhead press. One article cites Escamilla et al. (2002) for high RA, TrA and oblique activity during deadlifts. That primary claim was not verified. — [MuscleEvo](https://muscleevo.net/deadlift-work-abs/); [BoxRox](https://www.boxrox.com/?p=233238)
- **Coach view.** Squats and deadlifts build trunk strength but may not be enough for RA growth, so add a few direct ab sets per week. **[opinion/consensus]** — [EliteFTS "Do powerlifters need direct abdominal work"](https://elitefts.com/blogs/training/do-powerlifters-need-direct-abdominal-work); [NFPT](https://nfpt.com/develop-abs-without-ab-day/)

### Inferences
- For this lifter, with a squat around 365 and heavy pulling and pressing on 6 PPL days, bracing is already heavily trained. Direct ab work should target what the compounds under-train: RA flexion under load, external-oblique rotation and lateral flexion, and anti-extension with the abs at length. Low-load "stability" drills add little.

### Gaps
- No hypertrophy data were found on RA growth from compound lifts alone in trained lifters.

## Which exercises transfer to bracing and lifting performance (IAP, belts, anti-movement work, carries)

### Takeaway
Trunk or core training reliably increases trunk strength. Its transfer to maximal strength and athletic performance is small to moderate, and a powerlifter's main bracing skill is built by heavy lifting with a deliberate breath and brace (Valsalva) and a belt. Belts raise intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) by about 25–40% and can lower spinal compression, but only when the lifter inhales and braces against them. No study was found showing that a specific anti-rotation or carry exercise improves squat, bench or deadlift 1RM.

### Cited Findings
- **Prieske et al. 2016, Sports Medicine (meta-analysis).** Compared with no training or regular training, core strength training produced large effects on trunk muscle strength but only small-to-medium effects on physical fitness and athletic performance. — [Uni Potsdam PDF](https://www.uni-potsdam.de/fileadmin/projects/trainingswissenschaft/ATB-Ver%C3%B6ffentlichungen/Prieske_Muehlbauer_et_al_2016_SM.pdf); [PubMed 26589515](https://www.pubmed.gov/26589515/)
- **Saeterbakken, Prieske, Granacher et al. 2022 (meta-analysis, young and adult athletes).** Trunk muscle training had small-to-large effects: maximal strength SMD 0.39, local muscular endurance 1.29, lower-limb power 0.30, linear sprint 0.66. — [PMC9213339](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9213339/)
- **2025 meta-analysis (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil).** Core training improves foundational athletic qualities, but effects on sport-specific performance vary. — [PubMed 40319291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40319291/)
- **Belts and multi-rep squats (Lander et al. 1992).** IAP with a belt was consistently 25–40% higher than without (P<0.05). Erector spinae and external-oblique EMG did not differ, and the authors concluded the belt supports the trunk through higher IAP. — [PubMed 1533266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1533266/)
- **Belts and squat lifts (1990).** Erector spinae activity tended to drop when the breath was held, but the belt added no further reduction. The authors could not justify prescribing belts for short industrial lifts. — [PubMed 2141312](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2141312/)
- **Stiff belt in weightlifters (2006).** A stiff belt cut spinal compression by about 10%, but only when the lifter inhaled before lifting. The authors attributed this to a moment generated by the belt, not to IAP. — [PubMed 17047531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17047531/)
- **Belt and erector pressure (1999).** A belt raised erector spinae intramuscular pressure during Valsalva and maximal efforts without changing peak IAP. — [PubMed 10619094](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10619094/)
- **Belts during the squat (1990).** Lander et al. tested six experienced men at 70/80/90% 1RM to examine belt effects on spinal compression and muscle activity. — [PubMed 2709981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2709981/); [IAT Leipzig record](https://www.iat.uni-leipzig.de/datenbanken/iks/drv-rugby/Record/4000559)

### Inferences
- The belt literature does not support the claim that belts weaken the abs. Ab and oblique EMG were not lower with a belt in the multi-rep squat study. Using a belt on top sets and doing some beltless volume is a reasonable compromise, but this is **[opinion/consensus]**.
- For a powerbuilder, anti-extension work (ab wheel, long-lever plank, body saw) best matches the demand of resisting lumbar extension under a bar, e.g. bench arch control and the squat out of the hole. Anti-lateral-flexion work (suitcase carry, side plank) suits uneven loading and unilateral leg work. The Pallof press is a low-cost anti-rotation option. These are logical matches, not proven transfer. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No trial was found showing that adding Pallof presses, carries or rollouts raises powerlifting 1RMs in trained lifters.
- The Escamilla 2002 deadlift-trunk claim was not verified.

## How to progressively overload ab exercises (load, reps, ROM, lever, tempo, failure)

### Takeaway
No ab-specific dose-response or rep-range trials were found. The recommendations below come from general hypertrophy principles and coaching consensus. The practical approach: choose 1–2 loadable flexion movements (cable crunch, machine crunch, weighted decline sit-up) and progress them like an accessory lift, roughly 8–20 reps, 0–3 reps in reserve (RIR), adding load once the top of the rep range is reached. Progress anti-movement work by lever length, external load, or time and distance rather than by hold time alone.

### Cited Findings
- **Low-load, high-frequency ab work is a weak stimulus.** Vispute's 2×10 bodyweight protocol, 5 days a week, mainly improved endurance. — [Nassis](https://georgenassis.blogspot.com/2011/09/does-abdominal-exercise-training-reduce.html); [IDEA Fit](https://www.ideafit.com/crunches-donrsquot-cut-fat)
- **Instability is not the way to progress.** Ball exercises "may not provide enough stimulus for strength or hypertrophy", and loaded upright lifts produce more trunk activity than instability devices. — [Nuzzo 2008 full text](https://typeset.io/pdf/trunk-muscle-activity-during-stability-ball-and-free-weight-2pq4cyp0mo.pdf); [Hamlyn 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18076231/)
- **Loading options for weighted crunches.** Practical guides cover how much load to add and where to hold it (plate on chest vs behind the head, which also lengthens the lever). **[opinion/consensus]** — [Overload.coach weighted crunch guide](https://overload.coach/en/guides/weighted-crunches/); [Pocket-Fit cable crunch](https://www.pocket-fit.app/exercises/cable-rope-crunch)
- **Lengthened partials in trained lifters** produced adaptations similar to full ROM. This supports emphasizing the stretched portion of the crunch if full range is uncomfortable, though it is extrapolated from limbs. — [PMC11829627](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11829627/)

### Inferences (practical progression menu; [opinion/inference] unless noted)
- **Loaded flexion** (cable rope crunch, ab-crunch machine, weighted decline sit-up or crunch, stability-ball crunch with dumbbell):
  - 3–4 sets of 8–15 reps (up to about 20), stopping 0–2 RIR.
  - Use double progression: once all sets reach the top of the range, add 5–10 lb on the stack or a 2.5–5 kg plate.
  - Use a full range from slight spinal extension into full flexion, about 1 s pause in flexion and a 2–3 s eccentric.
  - Keep the hips fixed so the movement is spinal flexion, not hip flexion.
  - Decline-bench angle is another progression lever.
- **Hanging leg raise progression:** bent-knee raise, then knee raise with posterior pelvic tilt or curl, then straight-leg raise, then toes-to-bar, then an ankle weight or a dumbbell between the feet. A captain's chair reduces the grip demand. Train the pelvic curl, not just hip flexion; Escamilla flagged high rectus femoris activity in these movements.
- **Ab-wheel rollout progression:** kneeling to a wall, then kneeling full range, then kneeling with a band-resisted return or weight vest, then standing partial range, then standing full range. Progress range or lever before reps. Sets of about 5–12.
- **Anti-rotation and anti-lateral-flexion:**
  - Pallof press: progress cable load, distance from the stack, a split or tall-kneeling stance, then overhead press-outs.
  - Suitcase carry: progress load toward roughly 30–50% bodyweight per hand for 20–40 m, then distance.
  - Side plank: progress to feet-elevated or weighted, or the Copenhagen variant.
  - Cable side bend or landmine rotation adds dynamic oblique loading.
- **Frequency and volume for a 6-day PPL:** about 6–12 hard direct ab sets per week, spread over 2–4 sessions, is a reasonable default. Place them after the main lifts, or on push days, so trunk fatigue does not affect heavy squats and deadlifts.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed trials were found on ab rep ranges, proximity to failure, weekly volume, or tempo specifically for the abdominals. All dosing above comes from general hypertrophy principles.

## Spine safety debate around repeated spinal flexion

### Takeaway
McGill's caution against repeated flexion comes largely from in-vitro pig-spine studies that bent specimens thousands to tens of thousands of times under compressive load. Contreras & Schoenfeld (2011) reviewed this and concluded there is insufficient evidence that healthy people should avoid crunches. People with disc herniation, disc prolapse or flexion intolerance should avoid them. McGill and Schoenfeld later co-wrote an NSCA piece on choosing the crunch, which suggests a middle position: dose and individual tolerance matter.

### Cited Findings
- **Contreras & Schoenfeld 2011.** "To Crunch or Not to Crunch: An Evidence-Based Examination of Spinal Flexion Exercises…", *Strength and Conditioning Journal* 33(4):8–18, doi 10.1519/SSC.0b013e3182259d05. The review covers the risks and benefits of dynamic spinal flexion and its use in program design. — [Bret Contreras PDF](https://bretcontreras.com/wp-content/uploads/To-Crunch-or-Not-to-Crunch-An-Evidence-Based-Examination-of-Spinal-Flexion-Exercises-Their-Potential-Risks-and-Their-Applicability-to-Program-Design.pdf); [BISp record](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201306004416)
- **The cadaver and animal studies behind the concern** used total bending cycles of about 4,400 to 86,400 with compression around 1,500 N. — [Contreras & Schoenfeld PDF](https://bretcontreras.com/wp-content/uploads/To-Crunch-or-Not-to-Crunch-An-Evidence-Based-Examination-of-Spinal-Flexion-Exercises-Their-Potential-Risks-and-Their-Applicability-to-Program-Design.pdf)
- **Schoenfeld's later summary.** There is "simply not sufficient evidence to support the contention that healthy individuals should abstain from performing crunches". People with disc herniation, prolapse or flexion intolerance should avoid them. — [Look Great Naked blog](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/blog/evidence-based-core-training/); [LGN PDF](https://www.lookgreatnaked.com/articles/to_crunch_or_not_to_crunch.pdf)
- **Schoenfeld & McGill, NSCA *Personal Training Quarterly*, "Choosing Exercises—'The Crunch'".** The content is members-only and could not be retrieved. — [NSCA](https://nsca.com/education/articles/ptq/choosing-exercisesthe-crunch)
- **Claimed benefit.** Spinal flexion may improve nutrient delivery to the discs. This appears in a secondary source and was not verified against the primary review. — [Jim Stoppani](https://www.jimstoppani.com/training/to-crunch-or-not-to-crunch-that-is-the)
- **Hip-flexor load.** Escamilla flagged high rectus femoris activity in hanging knee-ups and rollouts as a possible concern for people with low-back problems. — [PubMed 16649890](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16649890/)

### Inferences (practical guidance; [opinion/inference])
- For a healthy lifter, moderate volumes of loaded flexion are defensible, e.g. 6–12 controlled sets a week. That is a tiny fraction of the cycle counts in the pig-spine studies, and the tissue presumably adapts in living people.
- Sensible precautions:
  - Avoid heavy flexion work right before heavy squats or deadlifts.
  - Avoid flexion work first thing after waking, when discs are most hydrated. This is a McGill recommendation, not verified this session.
  - Use controlled tempo rather than ballistic sit-ups.
  - If the lifter has a history of disc problems or flexion-related pain, replace loaded flexion with anti-extension and anti-lateral-flexion work plus isometric loaded crunch holds.

### Gaps
- No prospective trial was found linking crunch volume to disc injury in healthy lifters. The debate rests on in-vitro data and expert opinion.

## Gym machine and cable options commonly available

### Takeaway
Most commercial gyms have the equipment to load the abs progressively: a cable stack (rope crunch, Pallof, chops, side bends), a selectorized ab-crunch machine, a decline bench, a pull-up bar or captain's chair, and an ab wheel. Planks and stability-ball gadgets are the least loadable and score lower on EMG.

### Cited Findings
- The cable rope crunch is described as "the only loadable ab exercise most gyms have". **[opinion/consensus]** — [Pocket-Fit](https://www.pocket-fit.app/exercises/cable-rope-crunch); [Major Fitness](https://www.majorfitness.com/blogs/training-tips/cable-crunches)
- **Rollout devices.** The Ab Slide, Torso Track and Ab Roller produced RA activity comparable to the crunch in Escamilla's JOSPT study. — [PubMed 16494072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16494072/)
- **Hanging knee-ups, Power Wheel, inclined reverse crunch.** These gave the highest abdominal and hip-flexor activity in Escamilla's *Physical Therapy* study. — [PubMed 16649890](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16649890/)
- **Captain's chair.** It ranked top for the RA and obliques in the ACE study **[recalled, unverified this session]**. — [ACE PDF](https://www.acefitness.org/getfit/studies/bestworstabexercises.pdf)
- **Planks and side planks** produced less RA activity than the crunch in Stenger's thesis. — [MINDS@UW](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/13f23340-bc92-4049-9c47-6fcc60df9852/full)

### Inferences (candidate menu for a 6-day PPL; [opinion/inference])
- **Push days:** cable rope crunch or ab-crunch machine, 3×10–15, plus a Pallof press, 2×10–12 per side.
- **Pull days:** hanging knee or leg raise with pelvic curl, 3×8–15 (grip already trained that day), plus a suitcase carry, 2×30 m per side. Alternatively place the carry at the end of a leg day.
- **Leg days:** if any work is done, an ab-wheel rollout, 2–3×6–10, after squats or deadlifts, or skip direct work on heavy lower days.
- Rotate the flexion exercise every 6–12 weeks. Weighted decline sit-ups and stability-ball crunches are good choices for the stretched position.
